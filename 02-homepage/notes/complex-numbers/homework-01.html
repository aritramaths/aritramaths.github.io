☠ Problem 1.1

A dilation about OO sends a point at distance 88 to a point at distance 33. Find the scale factor. What happens to a segment of length 2020? Explain why your answer applies even if the segment does not pass through OO.

Show solutionHide solution

The scale factor is new distance divided by old distance, k=3/8k=3/8. Therefore a segment of length 2020 has image length 20(3/8)=15/220(3/8)=15/2. For any two endpoints, the triangles formed with the centre before and after dilation are similar, so their joining distance has the same scale factor. The segment itself need not pass through the centre.

☠ Problem 1.2

Convert [4,120∘][4,120^\circ] and [3,−90∘][3,-90^\circ] to Cartesian coordinates. Find a polar description with angle between 0∘0^\circ and 360∘360^\circ of (−3,−1)(-\sqrt3,-1).

Show solutionHide solution

Use (x,y)=(rcosθ,rsinθ)(x,y)=(r\cos\theta,r\sin\theta): [4,120∘]=(−2,23),[3,−90∘]=(0,−3).[4,120^\circ]=(-2,2\sqrt3),\qquad [3,-90^\circ]=(0,-3). For (−3,−1)(-\sqrt3,-1), the modulus is 3+1=2\sqrt{3+1}=2. Both coordinates are negative, so the point is in the lower-left quadrant. Its reference angle is 30∘30^\circ, because the coordinate magnitudes divided by 22 are 3/2,1/2\sqrt3/2,1/2. The required polar description is [2,210∘][2,210^\circ].

☠ Problem 1.3

Explain why (1,1)(1,1) and (−1,−1)(-1,-1) have the same ratio y/xy/x but different directions. Find a valid direction for each.

Show solutionHide solution

In each case y/x=1y/x=1, so tangent alone cannot distinguish the two rays. The first point lies in the upper-right quadrant and has direction 45∘45^\circ. The second lies in the lower-left quadrant and has direction 225∘225^\circ. Their rays are opposite, differing by a half-turn. The signs of both coordinates resolve the ambiguity.

☠ Problem 1.4

Compute [3,π/6][2,5π/6][3,\pi/6][2,5\pi/6]. Find the inverse of [2,5π/6][2,5\pi/6], and check your answer by multiplication.

Show solutionHide solution

Multiply distances and add directions: [3,π/6][2,5π/6]=[6,π],[3,\pi/6][2,5\pi/6]=[6,\pi], the real point −6-6. The inverse of [2,5π/6][2,5\pi/6] is [1/2,−5π/6][1/2,-5\pi/6]. Multiplying these gives [2(1/2),5π/6−5π/6]=[1,0]=1[2(1/2),5\pi/6-5\pi/6]=[1,0]=1. The inverse contracts by 22 and reverses the original turn.

☠ Problem 1.5

Find the scale and a clockwise or anticlockwise turn that carry [4,140∘][4,140^\circ] to [6,20∘][6,20^\circ] about OO. Verify by the product rule.

Show solutionHide solution

The scale is 6/4=3/26/4=3/2, and the change of direction is 20∘−140∘=−120∘20^\circ-140^\circ=-120^\circ, a clockwise turn. The instruction is [3/2,−120∘][3/2,-120^\circ], and [3/2,−120∘][4,140∘]=[6,20∘].[3/2,-120^\circ][4,140^\circ]=[6,20^\circ]. An anticlockwise turn of 240∘240^\circ has the same final direction.

☠ Problem 1.6

Prove associativity when one or more of the three factors is 00. Why must the argument for inverses exclude 00?

Show solutionHide solution

If any of P,Q,RP,Q,R is 00, then both (PQ)R(PQ)R and P(QR)P(QR) are 00: whichever intermediate product first contains the zero factor is zero, and every subsequent product remains zero. Thus associativity holds in every zero case. An inverse would require 0Q=10Q=1, but the zero-product rule gives 0Q=00Q=0 for every QQ. Since the origin and the unit point are distinct, no inverse exists.

☠ Problem 1.7

Find every point PP satisfying P2=1P^2=1, and every point satisfying P2=−1P^2=-1. Work with distances and directions, without using ii.

Show solutionHide solution

A solution cannot be zero. Write P=[r,θ]P=[r,\theta] with 0∘≤θ<360∘0^\circ\le\theta<360^\circ. In both equations r2=1r^2=1, so r=1r=1. For P2=1P^2=1, the doubled direction is a complete number of turns. The possibilities in this range are θ=0∘,180∘\theta=0^\circ,180^\circ, giving the real points 1,−11,-1. For P2=−1P^2=-1, the doubled direction ends at 180∘180^\circ, so θ=90∘,270∘\theta=90^\circ,270^\circ. These are the upper and lower unit points on the vertical axis.

☠ Problem 1.8

Can reflection in the horizontal axis be multiplication by a fixed point? Consider what such a multiplication would have to do to 11 and to J=[1,90∘]J=[1,90^\circ].

Show solutionHide solution

Reflection fixes 11. If it were multiplication by a fixed point UU, then U⋅1=1U\cdot1=1, forcing U=1U=1. But multiplication by 11 fixes every point, whereas reflection sends J=[1,90∘]J=[1,90^\circ] to [1,−90∘][1,-90^\circ], a different point. Therefore reflection cannot be multiplication by a fixed point. The distinction is also one of orientation: reflection reverses it, and nonzero multiplication preserves it.
