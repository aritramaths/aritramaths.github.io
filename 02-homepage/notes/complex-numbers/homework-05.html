☠ Problem 5.1

Let DD lie on BCBC with BD:DC=2:5BD:DC=2:5. If [ABC]=42[ABC]=42, find [ABD][ABD] and [ADC][ADC]. If PP lies on ADAD with AP:PD=3:1AP:PD=3:1, find [PBC][PBC].

Show solutionHide solution

The common height gives [ABD]:[ADC]=2:5[ABD]:[ADC]=2:5. Their sum is 4242, so the areas are 42(2/7)=1242(2/7)=12 and 42(5/7)=3042(5/7)=30. Since AP:PD=3:1AP:PD=3:1, the fraction PD/ADPD/AD is 1/41/4. The common-side comparison then gives [PBC]=PDAD[ABC]=14⋅42=212.[PBC]=\frac{PD}{AD}[ABC]=\frac14\cdot42=\frac{21}{2}.

☠ Problem 5.2

Let a=2+ia=2+i and b=8+7ib=8+7i. Find the point dividing ABAB in the ratio AP:PB=2:1AP:PB=2:1. Verify the answer by subtracting endpoint coordinates.

Show solutionHide solution

The section formula gives p=a+2b3=2+i+16+14i3=6+5i.p=\frac{a+2b}{3}=\frac{2+i+16+14i}{3}=6+5i. Then p−a=4+4ip-a=4+4i and b−p=2+2ib-p=2+2i. The first displacement is twice the second and points in the same direction, so the point lies between the endpoints and satisfies AP:PB=2:1AP:PB=2:1.

☠ Problem 5.3

Masses 2,3,52,3,5 are placed at vertices with coordinates 0,6,4i0,6,4i. Find their balance point and its normalised barycentric coordinates. Find the ratio in which the cevian from AA divides BCBC.

Show solutionHide solution

The total mass is 1010, and the balance point is p=2⋅0+3⋅6+5⋅4i10=95+2i.p=\frac{2\cdot0+3\cdot6+5\cdot4i}{10}=\frac95+2i. The normalised weights are (1/5,3/10,1/2)(1/5,3/10,1/2). The cevian from AA meets BCBC at the balance point of masses 33 at BB and 55 at CC. Therefore BD:DC=5:3BD:DC=5:3. The masses and the adjacent segment lengths appear in reverse order.

☠ Problem 5.4

In triangle ABCABC, let D∈ABD\in AB, E∈BCE\in BC satisfy AD:DB=2:3AD:DB=2:3 and BE:EC=4:5BE:EC=4:5. Let G=AE∩CDG=AE\cap CD and let BGBG meet ACAC at FF. Find AF:FCAF:FC and FG:GBFG:GB, showing compatible masses.

Show solutionHide solution

The first ratio AD:DB=2:3AD:DB=2:3 allows masses 33 at AA and 22 at BB. To obtain BE:EC=4:5BE:EC=4:5, the mass at CC must satisfy 2⋅4=mC⋅52\cdot4=m_C\cdot5, giving mC=8/5m_C=8/5. Multiply all masses by 55 to obtain 15,10,815,10,8 at A,B,CA,B,C.

The balance point on ACAC has total mass 2323 and AF:FC=8:15AF:FC=8:15. The total balance point GG is on AEAE and CDCD, so it is the given intersection. Combining A,CA,C first also puts it on BFBF. The masses at B,FB,F are 10,2310,23, and 10BG=23GF10\,BG=23\,GF. Thus FG:GB=10:23FG:GB=10:23.

☠ Problem 5.5

For a=0a=0, b=4b=4, c=3ic=3i, find the normalised barycentric coordinates of p=1+ip=1+i. Determine the three opposite area fractions.

Show solutionHide solution

Write 1+i=λ⋅0+μ⋅4+ν⋅3i1+i=\lambda\cdot0+\mu\cdot4+\nu\cdot3i. Equality of real and imaginary parts gives μ=1/4\mu=1/4 and ν=1/3\nu=1/3. Therefore λ=1−1/4−1/3=5/12\lambda=1-1/4-1/3=5/12. The normalised weights and opposite area fractions are (512,312,412).\left(\frac5{12},\frac3{12},\frac4{12}\right). The positive weights confirm that the point is inside the triangle. Its three subtriangle areas are in the ratio 5:3:45:3:4.

☠ Problem 5.6

For the same reference triangle, find the weights of p=5+ip=5+i. Explain geometrically why at least one weight is negative.

Show solutionHide solution

Similarly, μ=5/4\mu=5/4, ν=1/3\nu=1/3, so λ=1−5/4−1/3=−7/12\lambda=1-5/4-1/3=-7/12. The weights are (−7/12,15/12,4/12)(-7/12,15/12,4/12). The side BCBC has equation x/4+y/3=1x/4+y/3=1; the point (5,1)(5,1) has x/4+y/3=19/12>1x/4+y/3=19/12>1, so it lies beyond that side. The negative weight opposite BCBC records this position outside the triangle.

☠ Problem 5.7

Use Ceva's theorem to decide whether cevians with side ratios BD/DC=2BD/DC=2, CE/EA=3/4CE/EA=3/4 and AF/FB=2/3AF/FB=2/3 are concurrent. Find proportional vertex masses when they are.

Show solutionHide solution

The product is 2⋅(3/4)⋅(2/3)=12\cdot(3/4)\cdot(2/3)=1, so Ceva's theorem gives concurrence. Compatible masses at A,B,CA,B,C are 3,2,43,2,4: they give BD/DC=4/2=2,CE/EA=3/4,AF/FB=2/3.BD/DC=4/2=2,\quad CE/EA=3/4,\quad AF/FB=2/3. Their common balance point has barycentric coordinates (3:2:4)(3:2:4).

☠ Problem 5.8

Find the area of the triangle with coordinates 1+i1+i, 5+2i5+2i, 2+6i2+6i by a complex product. Check the sign corresponding to the listed vertex order.

Show solutionHide solution

Translate the first vertex to the origin. The two side arrows are 4+i4+i and 1+5i1+5i. Then 4+i¯(1+5i)=(4−i)(1+5i)=4+20i−i−5i2=9+19i.\overline{4+i}(1+5i)=(4-i)(1+5i)=4+20i-i-5i^2=9+19i. The signed area is half the imaginary part, 19/219/2. It is positive, so the listed order is anticlockwise. The ordinary area is also 19/219/2.

☠ Problem 5.9

An affine function has values 7,7,−27,7,-2 at the three vertices of a triangle. Find its largest value, describe all points attaining it, and find its value at the point with weights (2:3:5)(2:3:5).

Show solutionHide solution

The greatest vertex value is 77. If PP has normalised weights λ,μ,ν\lambda,\mu,\nu, then f(P)=7λ+7μ−2ν=7(1−ν)−2ν=7−9ν.f(P)=7\lambda+7\mu-2\nu=7(1-\nu)-2\nu=7-9\nu. For a point in the triangle, ν≥0\nu\ge0. Equality with 77 occurs exactly when ν=0\nu=0, which is the entire side joining the first two vertices, including its endpoints. At weights (2:3:5)(2:3:5), normalise by 1010 to get f(P)=2⋅7+3⋅7+5(−2)10=52.f(P)=\frac{2\cdot7+3\cdot7+5(-2)}{10}=\frac52.

☠ Problem 5.10

Prove that a direct similarity z↦uz+vz\mapsto uz+v, with u≠0u\ne0, preserves normalised barycentric coordinates relative to the image triangle. Identify the exact use of λ+μ+ν=1\lambda+\mu+\nu=1.

Show solutionHide solution

If p=λa+μb+νcp=\lambda a+\mu b+\nu c and the weights sum to 11, then up+v=u(λa+μb+νc)+(λ+μ+ν)v=λ(ua+v)+μ(ub+v)+ν(uc+v).\begin{aligned} up+v&=u(\lambda a+\mu b+\nu c)+(\lambda+\mu+\nu)v\\ &=\lambda(ua+v)+\mu(ub+v)+\nu(uc+v). \end{aligned} The expressions in parentheses are the image vertices. Thus the same weights represent the image of PP in the image triangle. The sum-to-one condition is used when replacing the translation term vv by (λ+μ+ν)v(\lambda+\mu+\nu)v. Since u≠0u\ne0, the image triangle remains nondegenerate, and uniqueness identifies these as its barycentric coordinates.

☠ Problem 5.11

A line meets BCBC at an interior point DD, CACA at an interior point EE, and the extension of ABAB at FF. Prove Menelaus's relation BDDCCEEAAFFB=1.\frac{BD}{DC}\frac{CE}{EA}\frac{AF}{FB}=1. Use perpendicular distances from the vertices to the line DEFDEF. Explain why this does not contradict Ceva's theorem.

Show solutionHide solution

Let hA,hB,hCh_A,h_B,h_C be the positive perpendicular distances from A,B,CA,B,C to line DEFDEF. The right triangles obtained by dropping these perpendiculars have the same acute angle with each original side line. Similarity therefore gives BDDC=hBhC,CEEA=hChA,AFFB=hAhB.\frac{BD}{DC}=\frac{h_B}{h_C},\qquad \frac{CE}{EA}=\frac{h_C}{h_A},\qquad \frac{AF}{FB}=\frac{h_A}{h_B}. For the first two comparisons, the vertices are on opposite sides of the transversal; for the last, A,BA,B are on the same side and FF is on the extension. The ratios use ordinary positive lengths in each case. Multiplication cancels all three heights and gives 11.

Ceva's theorem as stated in this course assumes that all three dividing points are inside their respective sides. Here FF is outside ABAB, so that hypothesis is absent. The same product of ordinary positive ratios describes a different configuration. Keeping the location of each point is essential when using unsigned lengths.
