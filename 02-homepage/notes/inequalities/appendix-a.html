Appendix A

Homogeneous Inequalities and the Normalization Method

Choose a scale, simplify a homogeneous expression and track equality.

This appendix develops normalization as a systematic method for homogeneous inequalities. It is written so that we can use it after the first three lectures, while the examples and exercises also prepare us for the later algebraic methods in this course. In Appendix B we shall combine normalization with the triangle substitution introduced in Section 1.3.

A.1  Why normalization appears in inequalities

Suppose we are asked to prove a2b+c+b2c+a+c2a+b≥a+b+c2,a,b,c>0.\frac{a^2}{b+c}+\frac{b^2}{c+a}+\frac{c^2}{a+b}\ge \frac{a+b+c}{2}, \qquad a,b,c>0. A natural first question is not “Which inequality should I apply?” Instead, ask:

Does the absolute size of a,b,ca,b,c matter, or only their ratios?

Replace (a,b,c)(a,b,c) by (λa,λb,λc)(\lambda a,\lambda b,\lambda c), where λ>0\lambda>0. The left-hand side becomes λ(a2b+c+b2c+a+c2a+b),\lambda\left(\frac{a^2}{b+c}+\frac{b^2}{c+a}+\frac{c^2}{a+b}\right), and the right-hand side also becomes λ(a+b+c)/2\lambda(a+b+c)/2. Thus a common rescaling does not alter the truth of the inequality.

So, if a+b+ca+b+c is merely carrying an irrelevant scale, we may choose that scale ourselves. Taking λ=1a+b+c\lambda=\frac{1}{a+b+c} allows us to work under a+b+c=1.\boxed{a+b+c=1}. This is the normalization method.

💡 Key idea

A homogeneous inequality does not distinguish between (a,b,c)(a,b,c) and (λa,λb,λc)(\lambda a,\lambda b,\lambda c). Normalization chooses one convenient representative from each such scaling family.

A.2  Homogeneous expressions

Definition A.2.1

An expression F(a,b,c)F(a,b,c) is homogeneous of degree dd if F(λa,λb,λc)=λdF(a,b,c)F(\lambda a,\lambda b,\lambda c)=\lambda^d F(a,b,c) for every λ>0\lambda>0.

For a polynomial, this is easy to check: every monomial must have the same total degree.

Example A.2.2

a3+b3+abca^3+b^3+abc is homogeneous of degree 33.

Example A.2.3

a3+b2+ca^3+b^2+c is not homogeneous, because its terms have degrees 3,2,13,2,1.

For rational expressions, subtract the degree of the denominator from the degree of the numerator. For instance, a2b+c\frac{a^2}{b+c} has degree 2−1=12-1=1, while ab(a+b)2\frac{ab}{(a+b)^2} has degree 2−2=02-2=0.

Hence ab+c+bc+a+ca+b\frac{a}{b+c}+\frac{b}{c+a}+\frac{c}{a+b} is homogeneous of degree 00: it is literally unchanged by common scaling.

A quick degree check

Before normalizing, I recommend writing the degree of each side mentally: (a+b+c)2⏟deg2(ab+bc+ca)2⏟deg4⇒deg6.\underbrace{(a+b+c)^2}_{\deg 2} \underbrace{(ab+bc+ca)^2}_{\deg 4} \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad \deg 6. If the other side is a product of three quadratic factors, it also has degree 66. That five-second check prevents many invalid “without loss of generality” steps.

A.3  The normalization principle

Theorem A.3.1 · Normalization principle

Suppose FF and GG are homogeneous of the same degree dd. Then for every λ>0\lambda>0, F(a,b,c)≥G(a,b,c)⇔F(λa,λb,λc)≥G(λa,λb,λc).F(a,b,c)\ge G(a,b,c) \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad F(\lambda a,\lambda b,\lambda c)\ge G(\lambda a,\lambda b,\lambda c). Consequently, any single positive homogeneous quantity may be fixed to a convenient positive constant, usually 11.

Proof

Since FF and GG have degree dd, F(λa,λb,λc)=λdF(a,b,c),G(λa,λb,λc)=λdG(a,b,c).F(\lambda a,\lambda b,\lambda c)=\lambda^dF(a,b,c), \qquad G(\lambda a,\lambda b,\lambda c)=\lambda^dG(a,b,c). Because λd>0\lambda^d>0, multiplying both sides by λd\lambda^d does not change the direction of the inequality.

More generally, if H(a,b,c)>0H(a,b,c)>0 is homogeneous of degree rr, choose λ=H(a,b,c)−1/r.\lambda=H(a,b,c)^{-1/r}. Then H(λa,λb,λc)=1.H(\lambda a,\lambda b,\lambda c)=1. This explains normalizations such as a+b+c=1,a2+b2+c2=1,abc=1,ab+bc+ca=1.a+b+c=1, \qquad a^2+b^2+c^2=1, \qquad abc=1, \qquad ab+bc+ca=1.

The geometric picture

Think of (a,b,c)(a,b,c) as a point in the positive octant. The points (λa,λb,λc),λ>0,(\lambda a,\lambda b,\lambda c),\qquad \lambda>0, lie on one ray through the origin. For a homogeneous inequality, all points on that ray represent the same essential configuration.

If we impose a+b+c=1a+b+c=1, we intersect every positive ray with the simplex a+b+c=1.a+b+c=1. If we impose a2+b2+c2=1a^2+b^2+c^2=1, we intersect the ray with the unit sphere.

Remark

This is why a three-variable homogeneous inequality has only two essential degrees of freedom. One degree of freedom is just scale, and normalization removes it.

A.4  How do we choose the normalization?

Homogeneity tells us that normalization is allowed. It does not tell us which normalization is useful.

💡 Key idea

Choose the normalization that makes the most complicated part of the problem disappear or creates a useful identity.

For example, under a+b+c=1a+b+c=1, b+c=1−a,c+a=1−b,a+b=1−c.b+c=1-a, \qquad c+a=1-b, \qquad a+b=1-c. Under a2+b2+c2=1a^2+b^2+c^2=1, 1−a2=b2+c2,1-a^2=b^2+c^2, and cyclically. These identities are often the real reason the normalization works.

A.5  Basic worked examples

Example 1: denominators suggest a+b+c=1a+b+c=1

Example A.5.1

For a,b,c>0a,b,c>0, prove a2b+c+b2c+a+c2a+b≥a+b+c2.\frac{a^2}{b+c}+\frac{b^2}{c+a}+\frac{c^2}{a+b} \ge \frac{a+b+c}{2}.

Both sides have degree 11, so the inequality is homogeneous. Since the denominators are b+c,c+a,a+bb+c,c+a,a+b, normalize a+b+c=1.a+b+c=1. Then b+c=1−a,c+a=1−b,a+b=1−c.b+c=1-a,\qquad c+a=1-b,\qquad a+b=1-c. Therefore a2b+c+b2c+a+c2a+b=a21−a+b21−b+c21−c.\frac{a^2}{b+c}+\frac{b^2}{c+a}+\frac{c^2}{a+b} = \frac{a^2}{1-a}+\frac{b^2}{1-b}+\frac{c^2}{1-c}. By Cauchy–Schwarz in Engel form, a21−a+b21−b+c21−c≥(a+b+c)2(1−a)+(1−b)+(1−c)=12.\frac{a^2}{1-a}+\frac{b^2}{1-b}+\frac{c^2}{1-c} \ge \frac{(a+b+c)^2}{(1-a)+(1-b)+(1-c)} =\frac12. Since a+b+c=1a+b+c=1, this is exactly the desired result.

Remark

The normalization did not prove the inequality by itself. It reorganized the denominators into 1−a,1−b,1−c1-a,1-b,1-c, after which Cauchy–Schwarz became obvious.

Example 2: product normalization

Example A.5.2

Prove (a+b+c)3≥27abc,a,b,c>0.(a+b+c)^3\ge 27abc, \qquad a,b,c>0.

Both sides have degree 33. Since the product is central, try abc=1.abc=1. Then the required inequality becomes a+b+c≥3,a+b+c\ge 3, which is exactly AM–GM: a+b+c3≥abc3=1.\frac{a+b+c}{3}\ge \sqrt[3]{abc}=1. Equality occurs at a=b=c=1a=b=c=1 in the normalized variables.

Example 3: the same inequality, a different normalization

We could instead impose a+b+c=1.a+b+c=1. Then the same inequality becomes abc≤127.abc\le \frac1{27}. Again AM–GM gives abc3≤a+b+c3=13.\sqrt[3]{abc}\le \frac{a+b+c}{3}=\frac13.

Both normalizations are legal. Neither is universally “correct”. The product normalization makes the equality point (1,1,1)(1,1,1); the sum normalization makes the target product a fixed numerical bound.

A.8  Best-constant problems: normalize the denominator

One of the most effective places to use normalization is a best-constant inequality. Suppose we want the smallest MM for which |F(a,b,c)|≤MG(a,b,c)|F(a,b,c)|\le M\,G(a,b,c) and F,GF,G have the same degree. If G>0G>0 away from the origin, normalize G=1G=1. Then the problem becomes an ordinary maximization problem for |F||F| on a compact-looking normalized set.

Proposition A.8.1 · IMO 2006 Shortlist, A6

Find the smallest real number MM such that |ab(a2−b2)+bc(b2−c2)+ca(c2−a2)|≤M(a2+b2+c2)2\left|ab(a^2-b^2)+bc(b^2-c^2)+ca(c^2-a^2)\right| \le M(a^2+b^2+c^2)^2 for all real a,b,ca,b,c.

Both sides have degree 44. Except at (0,0,0)(0,0,0), normalize a2+b2+c2=1.\boxed{a^2+b^2+c^2=1}. Then we are maximizing the absolute value of E=ab(a2−b2)+bc(b2−c2)+ca(c2−a2)E=ab(a^2-b^2)+bc(b^2-c^2)+ca(c^2-a^2) on the unit sphere.

The expression factorizes: E=(b−c)(a−b)(a−c)(a+b+c).E=(b-c)(a-b)(a-c)(a+b+c). By permutation symmetry we may assume a≤b≤ca\le b\le c. Hence |E|=(b−a)(c−b)(c−a)|a+b+c|.|E|=(b-a)(c-b)(c-a)|a+b+c|. Using (b−a)(c−b)≤(c−a2)2,(b-a)(c-b)\le \left(\frac{c-a}{2}\right)^2, we get |E|≤14(c−a)3|a+b+c|.|E|\le \frac14(c-a)^3|a+b+c|. \tag{8.1} Let D=(b−a)2+(c−b)2+(c−a)2,S=a+b+c.D=(b-a)^2+(c-b)^2+(c-a)^2, \qquad S=a+b+c. Weighted AM–GM gives (D3)3/2|S|≤(D+S24)2.\left(\frac D3\right)^{3/2}|S| \le \left(\frac{D+S^2}{4}\right)^2. But the identity D+S2=3(a2+b2+c2)=3D+S^2=3(a^2+b^2+c^2)=3 holds under our normalization. 💡 Key idea

When a best-constant problem already contains a positive homogeneous denominator such as (a2+b2+c2)2(a^2+b^2+c^2)^2, normalize that denominator. You are turning a ratio optimization into a constrained maximum.

A.9  Homogenization: running the movie backwards

Normalization and homogenization are opposite processes.

Suppose we are given a+b+c=1a+b+c=1 and asked to prove a2+b2+c2≥13.a^2+b^2+c^2\ge \frac13. The unrestricted homogeneous statement is a2+b2+c2≥(a+b+c)23.a^2+b^2+c^2\ge \frac{(a+b+c)^2}{3}. Likewise, under a+b+c=1a+b+c=1, 1−a=b+c.1-a=b+c. Thus the normalized expression a1−a\frac{a}{1-a} comes from the homogeneous expression ab+c.\frac{a}{b+c}. Similarly, a(1−a)a(1-a) is the normalized form of a(b+c).a(b+c).

Remark

Whenever an olympiad statement begins with a+b+c=1a+b+c=1, I want us to ask: “What homogeneous inequality is hiding behind the number 11?” This often reveals the intended structure.

A.10  Equality cases should influence the normalization

Suppose equality is expected at a=b=ca=b=c. Different normalizations place the equality point at different locations: NormalizationEqual-variable pointa+b+c=1(1/3,1/3,1/3)a2+b2+c2=1(1/3,1/3,1/3)abc=1(1,1,1)\begin{array}{c|c} \text{Normalization} & \text{Equal-variable point}\\ \hline a+b+c=1 & (1/3,1/3,1/3)\\ a^2+b^2+c^2=1 & (1/\sqrt3,1/\sqrt3,1/\sqrt3)\\ abc=1 & (1,1,1) \end{array} Sometimes this matters. If the proof will repeatedly use expansions around equality, the product normalization abc=1abc=1 may make the algebra cleaner. If denominators contain b+cb+c, the sum normalization is usually more valuable even though the equality point is less pretty.

A.11  Common mistakes

Mistake 1: normalizing a non-homogeneous statement

The quantities a2+b2+c2anda+b+ca^2+b^2+c^2 \quad\text{and}\quad a+b+c have different degrees. Therefore a statement comparing them is not automatically invariant under scaling. One cannot simply write “WLOG a+b+c=1a+b+c=1” unless an additional argument justifies it.

Mistake 2: choosing a legal but useless normalization

For ∑cyca2b+c≥a+b+c2,\sum_{\mathrm{cyc}}\frac{a^2}{b+c}\ge \frac{a+b+c}{2}, the condition abc=1abc=1 is legal, but it does not simplify the denominators. The condition a+b+c=1a+b+c=1 is much better.

Mistake 3: trying to impose two independent normalizations

There is only one scaling parameter λ\lambda. In general we may impose one condition such as a+b+c=1,a+b+c=1, but not simultaneously a+b+c=1andabc=1.a+b+c=1 \qquad\text{and}\qquad abc=1. The second condition would impose genuinely new information.

Mistake 4: forgetting the zero case

If we normalize by a2+b2+c2=1a^2+b^2+c^2=1, we have divided by a positive scale. The point (0,0,0)(0,0,0) must be handled separately. In most homogeneous inequalities it is immediate, but it should not be silently divided by zero.

Mistake 5: losing the equality case

When a proof uses several inequalities after normalization, track equality at each step. The normalization itself preserves equality configurations up to common scaling, but later estimates may introduce stronger restrictions.

A.12  A practical decision procedure

When I meet a homogeneous olympiad inequality, I usually follow this sequence.

  1. Check the degrees. Include denominators, radicals and products.

  2. Inspect the ugly part. Which repeated expression would become a constant after scaling?

  3. Inspect the constraint. A relation such as a3+b3=c3a^3+b^3=c^3 almost asks us to divide by c3c^3.

  4. Inspect the equality case. A normalization that puts equality at (1,1,1)(1,1,1) can simplify factorization.

  5. Predict the new identities. Before committing, ask what b+cb+c, 1−a21-a^2, ab+bc+caab+bc+ca, etc. will become.

  6. Only then choose the inequality tool. AM–GM, Cauchy, Jensen, uvwuvw, rearrangement, or factorization comes after the structural simplification.

💡 Key idea

Homogeneity tells us that normalization is allowed. The algebraic structure tells us which normalization is useful.

A.14  Summary sheet

For quick revision, remember the following chain:

Check homogeneity →\longrightarrow identify the natural scale →\longrightarrow normalize strategically
→\longrightarrow use the inequality tool

Typical signals are b+c appears repeatedly⇒a+b+c=1,a2+b2+c2 dominates⇒a2+b2+c2=1,multiplicative structure⇒abc=1,am+bm=cm⇒c=1 may be natural.\begin{aligned} b+c\text{ appears repeatedly} &\Longrightarrow a+b+c=1,\\ a^2+b^2+c^2\text{ dominates} &\Longrightarrow a^2+b^2+c^2=1,\\ \text{multiplicative structure} &\Longrightarrow abc=1,\\ a^m+b^m=c^m &\Longrightarrow c=1\text{ may be natural}. \end{aligned}

The deepest point is that normalization is not itself an inequality such as AM–GM or Cauchy–Schwarz. It is a way of choosing better coordinates for the problem. Once the scale is removed, the actual inequality often becomes much more transparent.
