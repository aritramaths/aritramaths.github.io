The AM–GM Inequality

Weights, equality conditions and choosing the right AM–GM grouping.

Sections on this page
3.1 The Arithmetic Mean–Geometric Mean Inequality3.2 The Two Extremal Meanings of AM–GM3.3 A First Strong Application: Nesbitt's Inequality3.4 Weighted AM–GM3.5 Basic Applications of AM–GM3.6 Choosing the Correct Form of AM–GM3.7 The Cauchy Reverse Technique

💡 Lecture aim

Develop the arithmetic mean–geometric mean inequality from its basic form to weighted versions and advanced applications. The main emphasis is not merely on applying AM–GM, but on choosing the terms and coefficients so that the required equality case can occur.

We already met AM–GM at the end of Lecture 2, where it appeared as a rather surprising consequence of rearrangement. Today I want to treat it as a method in its own right. The theorem is short, but a serious AM–GM proof usually depends on three decisions.

💡 Intuition · What We Are Really Learning Today

In a successful AM–GM argument we must decide

  1. which positive terms should be grouped together;

  2. how many copies of each term are needed, or equivalently which weights should be used;

  3. whether the equality conditions of those groups agree with the equality case expected in the original problem.

The inequality itself is only the engine. Most of the creativity lies in building the correct input for that engine.

3.1  The Arithmetic Mean–Geometric Mean Inequality

For positive real numbers, the arithmetic mean is never smaller than the geometric mean. This is one of the most frequently used principles in olympiad inequalities.

Theorem 3.1 · AM–GM Inequality

For all positive real numbers a1,a2,…,ana_1,a_2,\ldots,a_n, a1+a2+⋯+ann≥a1a2⋯ann.\frac{a_1+a_2+\cdots+a_n}{n} \ge \sqrt[n]{a_1a_2\cdots a_n}. Equality holds if and only if a1=a2=⋯=an.a_1=a_2=\cdots=a_n.

Proof by Cauchy's Forward–Backward Induction

The case n=2n=2 is already known: x+y2≥xy.\frac{x+y}{2}\ge\sqrt{xy}.

Assume the result is true for nn positive numbers. For a1,a2,…,a2n>0a_1,a_2,\ldots,a_{2n}>0, apply the induction hypothesis separately to the first and last nn terms: a1+⋯+a2n≥na1a2⋯ann+nan+1an+2⋯a2nn≥2na1a2⋯a2n2n,\begin{aligned} a_1+\cdots+a_{2n} &\ge n\sqrt[n]{a_1a_2\cdots a_n} +n\sqrt[n]{a_{n+1}a_{n+2}\cdots a_{2n}}\\ &\ge 2n\sqrt[2n]{a_1a_2\cdots a_{2n}}, \end{aligned} where the final step is the two-variable AM–GM inequality. Hence truth for nn variables implies truth for 2n2n variables. Starting from n=2n=2, the result follows for every power of 22.

We now prove the backward step. Suppose AM–GM is true for nn variables. Given a1,…,an−1>0a_1,\ldots,a_{n-1}>0, put s=a1+a2+⋯+an−1,an=sn−1.s=a_1+a_2+\cdots+a_{n-1}, \qquad a_n=\frac{s}{n-1}. Applying the nn-variable case gives s+sn−1≥na1a2⋯an−1sn−1n.s+\frac{s}{n-1} \ge n\sqrt[n]{a_1a_2\cdots a_{n-1}\frac{s}{n-1}}. Since the left side is ns/(n−1)ns/(n-1), simplification yields s≥(n−1)a1a2⋯an−1n−1.s\ge (n-1)\sqrt[n-1]{a_1a_2\cdots a_{n-1}}. Thus truth for nn variables implies truth for n−1n-1 variables. Combining the forward and backward steps proves the theorem for every natural number n≥2n\ge2.

At every stage, equality requires all the participating numbers to be equal. Therefore equality in the final inequality holds exactly when a1=a2=⋯=ana_1=a_2=\cdots=a_n.

Remark · Connection with Lecture 2

In Lecture 2, AM–GM was obtained from the rearrangement inequality by choosing special sequences. The present proof is logically independent and is useful because its forward–backward induction pattern reappears in many inequalities.

3.2  The Two Extremal Meanings of AM–GM

I have told you before that it is often easier to remember a theorem through the optimisation statement hidden inside it. AM–GM has two equivalent extremal interpretations.

Corollary 3.1 · Fixed Sum and Fixed Product Principles

For positive real numbers a1,…,ana_1,\ldots,a_n:

  1. if a1+⋯+an=Sa_1+\cdots+a_n=S is fixed, then a1a2⋯an≤(Sn)n;a_1a_2\cdots a_n\le \left(\frac Sn\right)^n;

  2. if a1a2⋯an=Pa_1a_2\cdots a_n=P is fixed, then a1+a2+⋯+an≥nPn.a_1+a_2+\cdots+a_n\ge n\sqrt[n]{P}.

In both cases equality occurs exactly when all the variables are equal.

Proof

Both conclusions are immediate rewritings of a1+⋯+ann≥a1a2⋯ann.\frac{a_1+\cdots+a_n}{n} \ge \sqrt[n]{a_1a_2\cdots a_n}. The first says that equal positive numbers maximise the product under a fixed sum; the second says that they minimise the sum under a fixed product.

Remark · Do Not Forget the Direction

Under a fixed sum, AM–GM gives an upper bound for a product. Under a fixed product, it gives a lower bound for a sum. Many incorrect solutions begin by remembering the correct formula but using it in the wrong optimisation direction.

3.3  A First Strong Application: Nesbitt's Inequality

Proposition 3.1 · Nesbitt's Inequality

For positive real numbers a,b,ca,b,c, ab+c+bc+a+ca+b≥32.\frac{a}{b+c}+\frac{b}{c+a}+\frac{c}{a+b}\ge\frac32. For positive real numbers a,b,c,da,b,c,d, ab+c+bc+d+cd+a+da+b≥2.\frac{a}{b+c}+\frac{b}{c+d}+\frac{c}{d+a}+\frac{d}{a+b}\ge2. Both statements extend to non-negative variables whenever the displayed fractions are defined, by continuity.

Proof

For the three-variable form, set S=ab+c+bc+a+ca+b,S=\frac{a}{b+c}+\frac{b}{c+a}+\frac{c}{a+b}, M=bb+c+cc+a+aa+b,N=cb+c+ac+a+ba+b.M=\frac{b}{b+c}+\frac{c}{c+a}+\frac{a}{a+b}, \qquad N=\frac{c}{b+c}+\frac{a}{c+a}+\frac{b}{a+b}. Clearly M+N=3M+N=3. By AM–GM, M+S=a+bb+c+b+cc+a+c+aa+b≥3,M+S =\frac{a+b}{b+c}+\frac{b+c}{c+a}+\frac{c+a}{a+b} \ge3, and similarly N+S≥3N+S\ge3. Hence M+N+2S≥6,M+N+2S\ge6, so 3+2S≥63+2S\ge6, proving S≥3/2S\ge3/2.

For four variables, put S=ab+c+bc+d+cd+a+da+b,S=\frac{a}{b+c}+\frac{b}{c+d}+\frac{c}{d+a}+\frac{d}{a+b}, M=bb+c+cc+d+dd+a+aa+b,M=\frac{b}{b+c}+\frac{c}{c+d}+\frac{d}{d+a}+\frac{a}{a+b}, N=cb+c+dc+d+ad+a+ba+b.N=\frac{c}{b+c}+\frac{d}{c+d}+\frac{a}{d+a}+\frac{b}{a+b}. Again M+N=4M+N=4. Also, M+S=a+bb+c+b+cc+d+c+dd+a+d+aa+b≥4.M+S =\frac{a+b}{b+c}+\frac{b+c}{c+d} +\frac{c+d}{d+a}+\frac{d+a}{a+b}\ge4. Moreover, N+S=a+cb+c+b+dc+d+a+cd+a+b+da+b≥4(a+c)a+b+c+d+4(b+d)a+b+c+d=4,\begin{aligned} N+S &=\frac{a+c}{b+c}+\frac{b+d}{c+d} +\frac{a+c}{d+a}+\frac{b+d}{a+b}\\ &\ge \frac{4(a+c)}{a+b+c+d} + \frac{4(b+d)}{a+b+c+d} =4, \end{aligned} where each pair was estimated by the two-variable Engel/AM–GM form. Therefore M+N+2S≥8M+N+2S\ge8, and since M+N=4M+N=4, we obtain S≥2S\ge2.

Equality case

For the three-variable inequality, equality occurs at a=b=ca=b=c. In the four-variable form, the source also records boundary equality configurations, for example a=ca=c, b=d=0b=d=0, and its cyclic counterparts, when the fractions are interpreted by continuity.

3.4  Weighted AM–GM

Proposition 3.2 · Weighted AM–GM Inequality

Let a1,a2,…,an>0a_1,a_2,\ldots,a_n>0, and let x1,x2,…,xn≥0x_1,x_2,\ldots,x_n\ge0 satisfy x1+x2+⋯+xn=1.x_1+x_2+\cdots+x_n=1. Then a1x1+a2x2+⋯+anxn≥a1x1a2x2⋯anxn.a_1x_1+a_2x_2+\cdots+a_nx_n \ge a_1^{x_1}a_2^{x_2}\cdots a_n^{x_n}.

Proof

It is enough first to understand the two-variable statement. Let x,y≥0x,y\ge0, x+y=1x+y=1, and a,b>0a,b>0. We claim that ax+by≥axby.ax+by\ge a^xb^y. Suppose first that x=mm+n,y=nm+n,x=\frac{m}{m+n}, \qquad y=\frac{n}{m+n}, where m,nm,n are non-negative integers. Applying ordinary AM–GM to mm copies of aa and nn copies of bb, ma+nb≥(m+n)ambnm+n.ma+nb \ge (m+n)\sqrt[m+n]{a^mb^n}. Dividing by m+nm+n gives ax+by≥axby.ax+by\ge a^xb^y.

For real weights, choose rational sequences rk→xr_k\to x and sk→ys_k\to y, with rk+sk=1r_k+s_k=1. The rational case gives ark+bsk≥arkbsk.ar_k+bs_k\ge a^{r_k}b^{s_k}. Taking limits yields the desired result. The general nn-variable form is obtained by the same rational approximation argument, using repeated copies of each aia_i when the weights are rational.

Equality case

If all weights are positive, equality in weighted AM–GM holds if and only if a1=a2=⋯=an.a_1=a_2=\cdots=a_n. Variables carrying weight 00 do not affect either side and therefore impose no equality restriction.

It is often more convenient not to normalise the weights in advance. If λ1,…,λn≥0\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_n\ge0 and Λ=λ1+⋯+λn>0\Lambda=\lambda_1+\cdots+\lambda_n>0, then λ1a1+⋯+λnanΛ≥a1λ1/Λ⋯anλn/Λ.\frac{\lambda_1a_1+\cdots+\lambda_na_n}{\Lambda} \ge a_1^{\lambda_1/\Lambda}\cdots a_n^{\lambda_n/\Lambda}. \tag{3.1}

Example 3.1 · Young's Inequality from Weighted AM–GM

Let p,q>1p,q>1 satisfy 1p+1q=1.\frac1p+\frac1q=1. For x,y>0x,y>0, apply weighted AM–GM to xp,yqx^p,y^q with weights 1/p,1/q1/p,1/q. We obtain xpp+yqq≥xy.\frac{x^p}{p}+\frac{y^q}{q}\ge xy. Equality holds exactly when xp=yqx^p=y^q. This is a good model of how a weighted inequality turns a product with awkward exponents into a sum.

Caution · AM–GM Needs Positive Inputs

Every term placed inside an AM–GM group must be non-negative, and roots or fractional powers require particular care. If an expression contains a negative term, first rewrite, move, or group the expression so that AM–GM is applied only to genuinely non-negative quantities.

3.6  Choosing the Correct Form of AM–GM

💡 Strategy · Let the Equality Case Choose the Coefficients

The most important habit in applying AM–GM is to choose terms and coefficients so that equality can occur at the expected equality point. A formally correct AM–GM estimate may be useless if its equality conditions are incompatible with the problem.

In the IMO Shortlist example above, the tempting estimate x3(1+y)(1+z)+(1+y)+(1+z)≥3x\frac{x^3}{(1+y)(1+z)}+(1+y)+(1+z)\ge3x is not suitable: at the expected equality point x=y=z=1x=y=z=1, the three terms are not equal. Instead, the coefficients 1/81/8 were chosen so that x3(1+y)(1+z),1+y8,1+z8\frac{x^3}{(1+y)(1+z)}, \qquad \frac{1+y}{8}, \qquad \frac{1+z}{8} all equal 1/41/4 when x=y=z=1x=y=z=1.

Remark · Balancing Coefficients

For symmetric problems whose equality occurs when all variables are equal, one can often group terms before applying AM–GM. In non-symmetric problems, the coefficients may need to be determined from a system of equations so that all AM–GM terms become equal at the desired equality point. This is often called balancing coefficients.

3.7  The Cauchy Reverse Technique

The following method rewrites one expression as a simpler main term minus a remainder. AM–GM is then applied to the denominator of the remainder. The minus sign reverses the direction in exactly the useful way.

💡 Method · The Basic Identity

For a,b>0a,b>0, a1+b2=a−ab21+b2.\frac{a}{1+b^2} =a-\frac{ab^2}{1+b^2}. Since 1+b2≥2b1+b^2\ge2b, a1+b2≥a−ab2.\frac{a}{1+b^2} \ge a-\frac{ab}{2}. The same idea extends to more complicated denominators.

💡 Lecture summary

AM–GM compares an arithmetic mean with a geometric mean and is proved for all numbers by Cauchy's forward–backward induction. Weighted AM–GM permits unequal coefficients. In applications, equality should guide the grouping and coefficient choice. The Cauchy reverse technique rewrites a rational expression as a main term minus an error term, after which AM–GM estimates the denominator in the useful direction.
