Appendix B

Ravi Transformation in Triangle Inequalities

Translate triangle geometry into inequalities for positive tangent lengths.

Sections on this page

In Section 1.3 we saw that three positive numbers a,b,ca,b,c are the side lengths of a non-degenerate triangle exactly when they can be written as a=y+z,b=z+x,c=x+y,x,y,z>0.a=y+z,\qquad b=z+x,\qquad c=x+y, \qquad x,y,z>0. At that stage we used the substitution only as one equivalent form of the triangle inequalities. Here we shall turn it into a systematic problem-solving method.

The central idea is simple: a constrained triple of triangle sides↔an arbitrary positive triple (x,y,z).\boxed{ \text{a constrained triple of triangle sides} \longleftrightarrow \text{an arbitrary positive triple }(x,y,z).} Once the triangle condition has been absorbed into positivity, the inequalities from the earlier lectures become available without further geometric constraints. In particular, we shall repeatedly return to AM–GM from Section 3.1, Cauchy–Schwarz from Section 1.6, and normalization from Appendix A.

B.1  Where the Substitution Comes From

Let s=a+b+c2s=\frac{a+b+c}{2} be the semiperimeter. The triangle inequalities say precisely that s−a>0,s−b>0,s−c>0.s-a>0,\qquad s-b>0,\qquad s-c>0. These are therefore the first positive quantities that we should try to use. Define x=s−a,y=s−b,z=s−c.\boxed{x=s-a,\qquad y=s-b,\qquad z=s-c.} Then y+z=(s−b)+(s−c)=2s−b−c=a,y+z=(s-b)+(s-c)=2s-b-c=a, and cyclically, a=y+z,b=z+x,c=x+y.\boxed{a=y+z,\qquad b=z+x,\qquad c=x+y.}

Proposition B.1 · The Ravi Correspondence

The following two sets are in one-to-one correspondence: {(a,b,c):a,b,c are the sides of a non-degenerate triangle}\left\{(a,b,c):a,b,c\text{ are the sides of a non-degenerate triangle}\right\} and {(x,y,z):x,y,z>0}.\left\{(x,y,z):x,y,z>0\right\}. The correspondence and its inverse are x=b+c−a2,y=c+a−b2,z=a+b−c2,a=y+z,b=z+x,c=x+y.\begin{aligned} x&=\frac{b+c-a}{2},& y&=\frac{c+a-b}{2},& z&=\frac{a+b-c}{2},\\ a&=y+z,& b&=z+x,& c&=x+y. \end{aligned}

Proof

If a,b,ca,b,c are triangle sides, all three half-differences in the first line are positive. Direct addition then gives the second line.

Conversely, if x,y,z>0x,y,z>0 and a=y+z,b=z+x,c=x+y,a=y+z,\qquad b=z+x,\qquad c=x+y, then b+c−a=2x>0,c+a−b=2y>0,a+b−c=2z>0.b+c-a=2x>0,\qquad c+a-b=2y>0,\qquad a+b-c=2z>0. Thus all three strict triangle inequalities hold. The two formulas plainly undo one another, so the correspondence is one-to-one.

💡 Intuition · Why This Is More Than a Trick

The substitution does not merely produce some convenient positive variables. It gives coordinates for the entire space of non-degenerate triangles. No triangle is lost, and every positive triple (x,y,z)(x,y,z) produces exactly one ordered triple of triangle sides.

B.2  The Incircle Interpretation

There is a geometric reason that the same variables appear so naturally. Let the incircle of △ABC\triangle ABC touch BC,CA,ABBC,CA,AB at D,E,FD,E,F, respectively. Tangent segments from the same external point are equal, so write AE=AF=x,BF=BD=y,CD=CE=z.AE=AF=x,\qquad BF=BD=y,\qquad CD=CE=z. If, as usual, a=BC,b=CA,c=AB,a=BC,\qquad b=CA,\qquad c=AB, then a=y+z,b=z+x,c=x+y.a=y+z,\qquad b=z+x,\qquad c=x+y.

Thus the Ravi variables are exactly the tangent lengths from the three vertices to the incircle. Algebraically they are s−a,s−b,s−cs-a,s-b,s-c; geometrically they are already present on the sides of the triangle.

B.3  The Ravi Dictionary

We should not memorize a long list of formulas. If we remember a=y+z,b=z+x,c=x+y,a=y+z,\qquad b=z+x,\qquad c=x+y, the rest can be reconstructed quickly. Still, it is useful to collect the most common translations in one place.

One particularly useful consequence is 2(ab+bc+ca)−(a2+b2+c2)=4(xy+yz+zx)>0.\boxed{ 2(ab+bc+ca)-(a^2+b^2+c^2)=4(xy+yz+zx)>0.} This identity converts a strict triangle inequality into the positivity of a very simple expression.

B.4  Area, Inradius, and Circumradius

Let Δ\Delta denote the area, rr the inradius, and RR the circumradius of the triangle.

Heron's formula and the Ravi dictionary give Δ2=s(s−a)(s−b)(s−c)=(x+y+z)xyz,\Delta^2=s(s-a)(s-b)(s-c) =(x+y+z)xyz, so Δ=xyz(x+y+z).\boxed{\Delta=\sqrt{xyz(x+y+z)}.} Because Δ=rs\Delta=rs, r2=Δ2s2=xyzx+y+z,r=xyzx+y+z.r^2=\frac{\Delta^2}{s^2} =\frac{xyz}{x+y+z}, \qquad \boxed{r=\sqrt{\frac{xyz}{x+y+z}}.} Finally, abc=4RΔabc=4R\Delta. Hence R=(x+y)(y+z)(z+x)4xyz(x+y+z).R=\frac{(x+y)(y+z)(z+x)} {4\sqrt{xyz(x+y+z)}}. The quotient R/rR/r is usually even more useful: Rr=(x+y)(y+z)(z+x)4xyz.\boxed{ \frac{R}{r} =\frac{(x+y)(y+z)(z+x)}{4xyz}.}

Remark · Derive, Do Not Memorize

The two lines s=x+y+z,s−a=x,s−b=y,s−c=zs=x+y+z, \qquad s-a=x, s-b=y, s-c=z immediately turn Heron's formula into the area formula. The identities for rr and RR then follow from Δ=rs\Delta=rs and abc=4RΔabc=4R\Delta. Remember this chain of ideas rather than four isolated formulas.

B.5  First Applications

Example B.1 · The Triangle Condition Becomes Positivity

Let a,b,ca,b,c be the sides of a non-degenerate triangle. Prove that a2+b2+c2<2(ab+bc+ca).a^2+b^2+c^2<2(ab+bc+ca).

Show solutionHide solution

By Proposition B.1, write a=y+z,b=z+x,c=x+ya=y+z,\qquad b=z+x,\qquad c=x+y with x,y,z>0x,y,z>0. The identity in Section B.3 gives 2(ab+bc+ca)−(a2+b2+c2)=4(xy+yz+zx)>0.2(ab+bc+ca)-(a^2+b^2+c^2)=4(xy+yz+zx)>0. This is exactly the required strict inequality.

Example B.2 · Three Triangle Differences

Let a,b,ca,b,c be the sides of a triangle. Prove that (a+b−c)(b+c−a)(c+a−b)≤abc.(a+b-c)(b+c-a)(c+a-b)\le abc.

Show solutionHide solution

After the Ravi substitution, the left-hand side becomes 8xyz8xyz, whereas abc=(x+y)(y+z)(z+x).abc=(x+y)(y+z)(z+x). By the two-variable AM–GM inequality from Section 3.1, x+y≥2xy,y+z≥2yz,z+x≥2zx.x+y\ge2\sqrt{xy},\qquad y+z\ge2\sqrt{yz},\qquad z+x\ge2\sqrt{zx}. Multiplying, (x+y)(y+z)(z+x)≥8xyz.(x+y)(y+z)(z+x)\ge8xyz. Equality holds exactly when x=y=zx=y=z, which is equivalent to a=b=ca=b=c.

Example B.3 · Unpleasant Denominators

Let a,b,ca,b,c be the sides of a triangle. Prove that ab+c−a+bc+a−b+ca+b−c≥3.\frac{a}{b+c-a} +\frac{b}{c+a-b} +\frac{c}{a+b-c} \ge3.

Show solutionHide solution

The denominators are the strongest possible signal for Ravi substitution. We obtain ∑cycab+c−a=12(y+zx+z+xy+x+yz)=12(xy+yx+yz+zy+zx+xz).\begin{aligned} \sum_{\mathrm{cyc}}\frac{a}{b+c-a} &=\frac12\left( \frac{y+z}{x}+\frac{z+x}{y}+\frac{x+y}{z} \right)\\ &=\frac12\left( \frac{x}{y}+\frac{y}{x} +\frac{y}{z}+\frac{z}{y} +\frac{z}{x}+\frac{x}{z} \right). \end{aligned} Each reciprocal pair is at least 22, by AM–GM. Therefore the sum is at least 33. Equality again requires x=y=zx=y=z, hence an equilateral triangle.

B.6  Two Classical Olympiad Applications

Example B.4 · IMO 1964, Problem 2

Let a,b,ca,b,c be the sides of a triangle. Prove that a2(b+c−a)+b2(c+a−b)+c2(a+b−c)≤3abc.a^2(b+c-a)+b^2(c+a-b)+c^2(a+b-c)\le3abc.

Show solutionHide solution

Put a=y+z,b=z+x,c=x+y.a=y+z,\qquad b=z+x,\qquad c=x+y. The required inequality becomes 2x(y+z)2+2y(z+x)2+2z(x+y)2≤3(x+y)(y+z)(z+x).2x(y+z)^2+2y(z+x)^2+2z(x+y)^2 \le3(x+y)(y+z)(z+x). After expansion, the right-hand side minus the left-hand side is x2y+x2z+xy2+xz2+y2z+yz2−6xyz.x^2y+x^2z+xy^2+xz^2+y^2z+yz^2-6xyz. Apply AM–GM to the first six positive terms: x2y+x2z+xy2+xz2+y2z+yz2≥6(x2y)(x2z)(xy2)(xz2)(y2z)(yz2)6=6xyz.\begin{aligned} &x^2y+x^2z+xy^2+xz^2+y^2z+yz^2\\ &\qquad\ge 6\sqrt[6]{(x^2y)(x^2z)(xy^2)(xz^2)(y^2z)(yz^2)} =6xyz. \end{aligned} Thus the difference is non-negative. Equality in the six-term AM–GM requires x=y=zx=y=z, so equality occurs precisely for an equilateral triangle.

Let a,b,ca,b,c be the sides of a triangle and Δ\Delta its area. Prove that a2+b2+c2≥43Δ.a^2+b^2+c^2\ge4\sqrt3\,\Delta.

Put p=x+y+zp=x+y+z and q=xy+yz+zx.q=xy+yz+zx. Then a2+b2+c2=2(x2+y2+z2+xy+yz+zx)=2(p2−q),a^2+b^2+c^2 =2(x^2+y^2+z^2+xy+yz+zx) =2(p^2-q), and Δ2=pxyz.\Delta^2=pxyz. B.7  Radius Inequalities in One Line

The formula for R/rR/r gives Euler's inequality immediately: Rr=(x+y)(y+z)(z+x)4xyz≥8xyz4xyz=2.\frac{R}{r} =\frac{(x+y)(y+z)(z+x)}{4xyz} \ge\frac{8xyz}{4xyz}=2. Thus R≥2r.\boxed{R\ge2r.} Equality holds exactly for an equilateral triangle.

Similarly, r2=xyzx+y+z.r^2=\frac{xyz}{x+y+z}. Since x+y+z=sx+y+z=s and xyz≤(s/3)3xyz\le(s/3)^3, r2≤s227.r^2\le\frac{s^2}{27}. Therefore s≥33r,\boxed{s\ge3\sqrt3\,r,} again with equality precisely for the equilateral triangle.

These short proofs explain why the Ravi substitution is so effective when R,r,sR,r,s, and Δ\Delta appear together: the geometric quantities become products and sums of ordinary positive variables.

B.8  Ravi Transformation Followed by Normalization

After Ravi substitution, a triangle inequality often becomes homogeneous in x,y,zx,y,z. At that point we may apply the method of Appendix A. For instance, x+y+z=s,x+y+z=s, so the normalization x+y+z=1x+y+z=1 is exactly the same as normalizing the original triangle by s=1⇔a+b+c=2.s=1 \qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad a+b+c=2.

💡 Method · A Two-Stage Workflow

For a homogeneous triangle inequality, try the following sequence.

  1. Replace the triangle sides by a=y+z,b=z+x,c=x+y.a=y+z,\qquad b=z+x,\qquad c=x+y.

  2. Check the degree of the resulting expression.

  3. If it is homogeneous, remove the scale by a useful normalization, commonly x+y+z=1x+y+z=1 or xyz=1xyz=1.

  4. Apply the standard inequality suggested by the new form.

  5. Translate the equality condition back to the triangle.

This is the conceptual bridge between the triangle discussion in Lecture 1 and the normalization appendix. Ravi removes the triangle constraints; normalization removes the irrelevant scale.

B.9  Recognition Signals and a Warning

Ravi substitution should come to mind when one or more of the following occurs:

  1. a,b,ca,b,c are explicitly the side lengths of a triangle;

  2. the expressions b+c−a,c+a−b,a+b−cb+c-a,c+a-b,a+b-c appear;

  3. the expressions s−a,s−b,s−cs-a,s-b,s-c appear;

  4. Heron's formula or the area Δ\Delta is involved;

  5. the quantities R,r,s,ΔR,r,s,\Delta occur together.

The second and third signals are especially strong because the relevant expressions become simply 2x,2y,2z2x,2y,2z or x,y,zx,y,z.

Caution · Do Not Substitute Mechanically

The right question is not “Can I use Ravi substitution?” but “Does Ravi remove the difficult part of this problem?” If a problem is fundamentally about angles and already contains sin(A/2)\sin(A/2), cosA\cos A, or tan(A/2)\tan(A/2), a trigonometric substitution may preserve the structure much better. We use a substitution to simplify the constraint, not to display a formula that we know.

B.10  Ravi Applied to New Triangle-Like Quantities

Sometimes the three quantities to which Ravi should be applied are not the original sides. If a,b,ca,b,c are triangle sides, then a+b>a+b>c,\sqrt a+\sqrt b>\sqrt{a+b}>\sqrt c, and cyclically. Hence a,b,c\sqrt a,\sqrt b,\sqrt c are themselves the side lengths of a non-degenerate triangle.

This observation is used in IMO 2006 Shortlist Problem A5. One introduces x=b+c−a,y=c+a−b,z=a+b−c.\begin{aligned} x&=\sqrt b+\sqrt c-\sqrt a,\\ y&=\sqrt c+\sqrt a-\sqrt b,\\ z&=\sqrt a+\sqrt b-\sqrt c. \end{aligned} These are twice the Ravi variables of the new triangle with sides a,b,c\sqrt a,\sqrt b,\sqrt c. The inverse relations are a=y+z2,b=z+x2,c=x+y2.\sqrt a=\frac{y+z}{2},\qquad \sqrt b=\frac{z+x}{2},\qquad \sqrt c=\frac{x+y}{2}. The point is broader than this one problem: whenever three expressions satisfy triangle inequalities, we may consider Ravi coordinates for those expressions. The complete shortlist problem appears as Exercise 12 below.

Let a,b,ca,b,c be the sides of a triangle. Prove that b+c−ab+c−a+c+a−bc+a−b+a+b−ca+b−c≤3.\frac{\sqrt{b+c-a}}{\sqrt b+\sqrt c-\sqrt a} +\frac{\sqrt{c+a-b}}{\sqrt c+\sqrt a-\sqrt b} +\frac{\sqrt{a+b-c}}{\sqrt a+\sqrt b-\sqrt c} \le3. Because a,b,ca,b,c are triangle sides, a<b+c.a<b+c. Therefore a<b+c<b+c.\sqrt a<\sqrt{b+c}<\sqrt b+\sqrt c. In particular, x=b+c−a,y=c+a−b,z=a+b−c\begin{aligned} x&=\sqrt b+\sqrt c-\sqrt a,\\ y&=\sqrt c+\sqrt a-\sqrt b,\\ z&=\sqrt a+\sqrt b-\sqrt c \end{aligned} are positive. Moreover, a=y+z2,b=z+x2,c=x+y2.\sqrt a=\frac{y+z}{2},\qquad \sqrt b=\frac{z+x}{2},\qquad \sqrt c=\frac{x+y}{2}.

We calculate b+c−a=(z+x2)2+(x+y2)2−(y+z2)2=x2−12(x−y)(x−z).\begin{aligned} b+c-a &=\left(\frac{z+x}{2}\right)^2 +\left(\frac{x+y}{2}\right)^2 -\left(\frac{y+z}{2}\right)^2\\ &=x^2-\frac12(x-y)(x-z). \end{aligned} Hence b+c−ab+c−a=1−(x−y)(x−z)2x2.\frac{\sqrt{b+c-a}}{\sqrt b+\sqrt c-\sqrt a} =\sqrt{1-\frac{(x-y)(x-z)}{2x^2}}. 💡 Summary · The Ravi Workflow

The triangle condition itself was never used after the substitution. It was encoded once and for all in x,y,z>0x,y,z>0. We then used AM–GM for products, Cauchy–Schwarz for reciprocal expressions, Engel form for a best-constant problem, and normalization for a homogeneous ratio. This is the role of Ravi coordinates: they connect triangle geometry to the ordinary inequality tools developed elsewhere in the book.
