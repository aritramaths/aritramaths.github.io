Introduction

How Level 2 Grows from Level 1

Part I developed the basic language of combinatorics: counting principles, permutations and combinations, bijections, inclusion–exclusion, distribution, and the Pigeonhole Principle. In Part II we shall assume that language.

The new question is still a counting question: How many objects satisfy the given conditions?\boxed{\text{How many objects satisfy the given conditions?}} But at Level 2 we allow ourselves a new strategy. Instead of insisting on a direct formula, we compare an object of size nn with smaller objects of the same kind.

This is why recurrence relations enter the course. I do not want recurrence relations to become a separate algebra chapter. Our purpose is much more concrete:

counting problem→good decomposition→recurrence→answer.\boxed{ \text{counting problem} \longrightarrow \text{good decomposition} \longrightarrow \text{recurrence} \longrightarrow \text{answer}. }

Only after we have learnt to create useful recurrences from counting problems shall we study a few basic ways of solving them. Generating functions then appear as another counting language: they allow us to encode many possible sizes at once, and they also give a systematic way to solve some recurrences.

The three lectures in this part are therefore designed as one continuous problem-solving story:

  1. Lecture 7: Counting by Recurrence. The central task is to create a recurrence from a counting problem. We use first-step and last-step decompositions, a distinguished element, states, tilings, strings, compositions, and Catalan-type decompositions.

  2. Lecture 8: Reading and Solving Counting Recurrences. We first read systems of recurrences from transition graphs, reduce them using symmetry, and only then introduce the solving methods that we need: iteration, telescoping, characteristic equations, and simple non-homogeneous recurrences.

  3. Lecture 9: Generating Functions as a Counting Tool. We build on the generating-function preview from Level 1. The emphasis is on encoding choices, extracting coefficients, solving counting recurrences, and understanding Catalan and partition-type constructions.

Remark

This is Level 2 material. I shall therefore not stop every time we use a binomial coefficient, a bijection, or the Pigeonhole Principle. We have already built those tools in Part I. What I shall do instead is point out the new structural step in each argument.
