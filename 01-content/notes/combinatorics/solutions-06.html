Results to keep nearby.

Theorem 6.1 (Pigeonhole Principle); Theorem 6.2 (generalized Pigeonhole Principle); Theorem 6.4 (A Consecutive Block with Divisible Sum); Theorem 6.6 (monotone subsequences)

In every solution we explicitly identify the pigeons and the holes. I also explain what a collision means in the language of the original problem. That translation–from “same hole” to equal remainders, a short distance, a repeated value, or a forced configuration–is the main problem-solving habit of Lecture 6.

What to notice.

The Pigeonhole Principle is usually easy after the holes have been chosen. The creative step is to choose a classification for which two objects in the same class give exactly the conclusion we want.
