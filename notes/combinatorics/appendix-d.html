Why a Second Kind of Generating Function?

An ordinary generating function stores ana_n as the coefficient of xnx^n. For labelled objects, choosing which labels belong to which component introduces binomial coefficients. Dividing the coefficient by n!n! makes those label choices interact cleanly with multiplication.

Definition D.1

Exponential Generating Function

The exponential generating function, abbreviated EGF, of a sequence (an)n≥0(a_n)_{n\ge0} is Â(x)=∑n≥0anxnn!.\boxed{ \widehat A(x)=\sum_{n\ge0}a_n\frac{x^n}{n!}. }

Examples: ∑n≥0xnn!=ex\sum_{n\ge0}\frac{x^n}{n!}=e^x is the EGF of 1,1,1,…1,1,1,\ldots, while ∑n≥0n!xnn!=11−x\sum_{n\ge0}n!\frac{x^n}{n!}=\frac1{1-x} is the EGF of 0!,1!,2!,…0!,1!,2!,\ldots.

Lecture 8 introduced S(n,k)=#{partitions of [n] into k nonempty blocks}.S(n,k)=\#\{\text{partitions of }[n]\text{ into }k\text{ nonempty blocks}\}. A single nonempty labelled block has EGF ex−1e^x-1. Oscillating Permutations: Advanced Enrichment

A permutation e1e2⋯ene_1e_2\cdots e_n of [n][n] is called oscillating or alternating if e1<e2>e3<e4>⋯.e_1<e_2>e_3<e_4>\cdots. The first numbers of such permutations are 1,1,2,5,16,61,272,1385,….1,1,2,5,16,61,272,1385,\ldots.

Separate odd and even lengths. Let ana_n be the number for odd nn and zero for even nn, while bnb_n is the number for even nn, with b0=1b_0=1 and zero for odd nn. Splitting an alternating permutation at its largest entry gives the convolution recurrences an=∑k=0n−1(n−1k)akan−1−k(n odd),a_n=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\binom{n-1}{k}a_ka_{n-1-k} \qquad(n\text{ odd}), and bn=∑k=0n−1(n−1k)akbn−1−k(n even).b_n=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\binom{n-1}{k}a_kb_{n-1-k} \qquad(n\text{ even}).

Define the EGFs A(x)=∑n≥0anxnn!,B(x)=∑n≥0bnxnn!.A(x)=\sum_{n\ge0}a_n\frac{x^n}{n!}, \qquad B(x)=\sum_{n\ge0}b_n\frac{x^n}{n!}. The recurrences become A′(x)=1+A(x)2,B′(x)=A(x)B(x),A'(x)=1+A(x)^2, \qquad B'(x)=A(x)B(x), with A(0)=0A(0)=0 and B(0)=1B(0)=1. Hence A(x)=tanx,B(x)=secx.\boxed{A(x)=\tan x}, \qquad \boxed{B(x)=\sec x}. Therefore the EGF for all oscillating permutations is tanx+secx.\boxed{\tan x+\sec x}.

This example shows how an exponential generating function can solve a nonlinear recurrence containing binomial coefficients.
