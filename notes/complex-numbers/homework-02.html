☠ Problem 2.1

For z=2+3iz=2+3i and w=−1+2iw=-1+2i, find z+wz+w, z−wz-w, zwzw and z/wz/w. Interpret z−wz-w as a displacement.

Show solutionHide solution

Add and subtract components: z+w=1+5i,z−w=3+i.z+w=1+5i,\qquad z-w=3+i. The product is (2+3i)(−1+2i)=−2+4i−3i+6i2=−8+i.(2+3i)(-1+2i)=-2+4i-3i+6i^2=-8+i. For division, zw=(2+3i)(−1−2i)(−1+2i)(−1−2i)=−2−4i−3i−6i25=4−7i5.\frac zw=\frac{(2+3i)(-1-2i)}{(-1+2i)(-1-2i)} =\frac{-2-4i-3i-6i^2}{5}=\frac{4-7i}{5}. The difference z−wz-w is the displacement from the point with coordinate ww to the point with coordinate zz: adding 3+i3+i to −1+2i-1+2i gives 2+3i2+3i.

☠ Problem 2.2

Rotate 3+i3+i anticlockwise through 90∘90^\circ about 1−i1-i. Show all three stages of the calculation.

Show solutionHide solution

Subtract the centre: (3+i)−(1−i)=2+2i(3+i)-(1-i)=2+2i. Multiply by ii for an anticlockwise quarter-turn: i(2+2i)=−2+2ii(2+2i)=-2+2i. Add the centre back: (1−i)+(−2+2i)=−1+i.(1-i)+(-2+2i)=-1+i. The original and final displacements from the centre both have modulus 222\sqrt2, as required for a rotation.

☠ Problem 2.3

A spiral similarity has centre 2+i2+i, scale factor 22 and clockwise angle 90∘90^\circ. Find the image of 3+3i3+3i, and check the ratio of distances from the centre.

Show solutionHide solution

The multiplier for scale 22 and a clockwise quarter-turn is −2i-2i. Hence z′=(2+i)−2i((3+3i)−(2+i))=(2+i)−2i(1+2i)=6−i.z'=(2+i)-2i\bigl((3+3i)-(2+i)\bigr) =(2+i)-2i(1+2i)=6-i. The old displacement is 1+2i1+2i, of modulus 5\sqrt5. The new one is (6−i)−(2+i)=4−2i(6-i)-(2+i)=4-2i, of modulus 20=25\sqrt{20}=2\sqrt5. Their quotient is −2i-2i, recording both the scale and the turn.

☠ Problem 2.4

Find the direct similarity carrying 11 to ii and 33 to 4+i4+i. Is it a translation? Find its centre if it has one.

Show solutionHide solution

The multiplier is u=(4+i)−i3−1=2.u=\frac{(4+i)-i}{3-1}=2. The image of 11 gives i=2+vi=2+v, so v=−2+iv=-2+i and the transformation is z′=2z−2+iz'=2z-2+i. Since u≠1u\ne1, it is not a translation. Its centre is p=−2+i1−2=2−i.p=\frac{-2+i}{1-2}=2-i. Indeed, z′=(2−i)+2(z−(2−i))z'=(2-i)+2(z-(2-i)), a dilation by 22 about 2−i2-i.

☠ Problem 2.5

Find Arg(−3+i)\operatorname{Arg}(-\sqrt3+i) and Arg(−1−3i)\operatorname{Arg}(-1-\sqrt3 i) in degrees. Find the principal argument of their product without multiplying out.

Show solutionHide solution

The point −3+i-\sqrt3+i lies in the upper-left quadrant with reference angle 30∘30^\circ, so its principal argument is 150∘150^\circ. The point −1−3i-1-\sqrt3 i lies in the lower-left quadrant with direction 240∘240^\circ, represented in the principal range by −120∘-120^\circ. Their product has argument 150∘−120∘=30∘150^\circ-120^\circ=30^\circ, already in the principal range.

☠ Problem 2.6

Prove that |z−1|=2|z+1|\left|z-1\right|=2\left|z+1\right| describes a circle, and find its centre and radius.

Show solutionHide solution

Write z=x+iyz=x+iy. Squaring the two nonnegative distances gives (x−1)2+y2=4((x+1)2+y2).(x-1)^2+y^2=4((x+1)^2+y^2). Expansion and rearrangement yield 3x2+10x+3+3y2=0.3x^2+10x+3+3y^2=0. Divide by 33 and complete the square: (x+53)2+y2=259−1=169.\left(x+\frac53\right)^2+y^2=\frac{25}{9}-1=\frac{16}{9}. This is the circle with centre −5/3-5/3 and radius 4/34/3. Squaring introduced no extra solutions because both sides of the original equation were nonnegative.

☠ Problem 2.7

Suppose a≠ba\ne b, c≠dc\ne d and d−c=−i(b−a)d-c=-i(b-a). State and prove the two geometric conclusions this gives about ABAB and CDCD.

Show solutionHide solution

Multiplication by −i-i turns an arrow clockwise by 90∘90^\circ and preserves its modulus. Thus the arrow CDCD is perpendicular to ABAB, and CD=|d−c|=|−i||b−a|=|b−a|=AB.CD=\left|d-c\right|=\left|-i\right|\left|b-a\right|=\left|b-a\right|=AB. The assumptions ensure that both segments have a nonzero length, so both directions are defined.

☠ Problem 2.8

Reflect 3+5i3+5i in the line y=x+1y=x+1 by translating, rotating, conjugating and reversing the first two steps.

Show solutionHide solution

The line y=x+1y=x+1 passes through p=ip=i and makes angle 45∘45^\circ with the real axis. Begin with z=3+5iz=3+5i. Subtract ii to obtain 3+4i3+4i. Rotate clockwise through 45∘45^\circ: 1−i2(3+4i)=7+i2.\frac{1-i}{\sqrt2}(3+4i)=\frac{7+i}{\sqrt2}. Conjugate to get (7−i)/2(7-i)/\sqrt2. Rotate back: 1+i27−i2=8+6i2=4+3i.\frac{1+i}{\sqrt2}\frac{7-i}{\sqrt2} =\frac{8+6i}{2}=4+3i. Finally add ii, giving 4+4i4+4i. Its midpoint with 3+5i3+5i is 7/2+(9/2)i7/2+(9/2)i, on the reflecting line, and their joining segment has slope −1-1, perpendicular to that line.

☠ Problem 2.9

Prove the parallelogram identity by expanding both squared moduli. Explain what each of its four lengths represents in a diagram.

Show solutionHide solution

Expand with conjugates: |z+w|2=|z|2+|w|2+zw‾+z‾w,|z−w|2=|z|2+|w|2−zw‾−z‾w.\begin{aligned} \left|z+w\right|^2&=\left|z\right|^2+\left|w\right|^2+z\bar w+\bar z w,\\ \left|z-w\right|^2&=\left|z\right|^2+\left|w\right|^2-z\bar w-\bar z w. \end{aligned} Addition cancels the cross terms, giving the stated identity. In the parallelogram with successive vertices 0,z,z+w,w0,z,z+w,w, the diagonals have lengths |z+w|\left|z+w\right| and |z−w|\left|z-w\right|; its four sides have lengths |z|,|w|,|z|,|w|\left|z\right|,\left|w\right|,\left|z\right|,\left|w\right|. The equation compares the sum of the two squared diagonal lengths with the sum of the four squared side lengths.

☠ Problem 2.10

Find all zz satisfying |z|+|z−4|=4\left|z\right|+\left|z-4\right|=4. Give both a geometric argument and an explanation of the equality condition in the triangle inequality.

Show solutionHide solution

The expression |z|+|z−4|\left|z\right|+\left|z-4\right| is the length of the route from 00 to zz to 44. It equals the direct distance 44 precisely when zz lies on the segment from 00 to 44. Thus the solution set is the real interval 0≤z≤40\le z\le4.

Algebraically, 4=z+(4−z)4=z+(4-z), so the triangle inequality gives 4≤|z|+|4−z|4\le\left|z\right|+\left|4-z\right|. If neither summand is zero, equality requires zz and 4−z4-z to point in the same direction. Their sum is positive real, so both must be positive real. This gives 0<z<40<z<4. The two zero-summand cases supply the endpoints.

☠ Problem 2.11

Let A=1+iA=1+i and B=3+2iB=3+2i. Find the point PP for which APAP is twice as long as ABAB and is obtained from the arrow ABAB by a clockwise quarter-turn. Write the displacement equation first, then calculate pp.

Show solutionHide solution

The arrow ABAB has coordinate b−a=(3+2i)−(1+i)=2+ib-a=(3+2i)-(1+i)=2+i. A clockwise quarter-turn is multiplication by −i-i, and doubling is multiplication by 22. Thus the required displacement equation is p−a=−2i(b−a).p-a=-2i(b-a). Substitute the known coordinates and expand: p=(1+i)−2i(2+i)=1+i−4i−2i2=3−3i.p=(1+i)-2i(2+i)=1+i-4i-2i^2=3-3i. The multiplier −2i-2i has modulus 22 and turns clockwise through 90∘90^\circ. Indeed |p−a|=|2−4i|=25=2|b−a|\left|p-a\right|=\left|2-4i\right|=2\sqrt5=2\left|b-a\right|.
