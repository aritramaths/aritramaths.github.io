☠ Problem 3.1

Find the centres of the two squares on the side joining 2−i2-i to 5+i5+i. Derive one centre by rotating about the unknown centre, not just by quoting the formula.

Show solutionHide solution

For the left square centre ss, the quarter-turn equation is b−s=i(a−s)b-s=i(a-s). Hence (i−1)s=ia−b(i-1)s=ia-b and s=a+b+i(b−a)2.s=\frac{a+b+i(b-a)}2. Here (a+b)/2=7/2(a+b)/2=7/2 and i(b−a)/2=i(3+2i)/2=−1+3i/2i(b-a)/2=i(3+2i)/2=-1+3i/2. The left centre is 5/2+3i/25/2+3i/2; changing the sign of the perpendicular term gives the right centre 9/2−3i/29/2-3i/2. In each case, the distances to the two side endpoints agree.

☠ Problem 3.2

A clockwise square has vertices a=ia=i, b=1+ib=1+i, c=1c=1, d=0d=0. Construct its four exterior squares, find their centres, and verify the opposite-centre quarter-turn identity numerically.

Show solutionHide solution

Clockwise order puts each exterior square on the left. The four centre formulas give s1=12+32i,s2=32+12i,s3=12−12i,s4=−12+12i.s_1=\tfrac12+\tfrac32i,\quad s_2=\tfrac32+\tfrac12i, \quad s_3=\tfrac12-\tfrac12i,\quad s_4=-\tfrac12+\tfrac12i. Then s1−s3=2is_1-s_3=2i and s2−s4=2s_2-s_4=2, so s1−s3=i(s2−s4)s_1-s_3=i(s_2-s_4). The two segments are vertical and horizontal, each of length 22.

☠ Problem 3.3

In the mixed-angle construction, take a=ia=i, b=0b=0, c=1c=1. Compute p,q,rp,q,r exactly and verify q−r=i(p−r)q-r=i(p-r).

☠ Problem 3.4

Find the two possible third vertices of an equilateral triangle whose first two vertices are 11 and ii.

Show solutionHide solution

The midpoint is (1+i)/2(1+i)/2. The side arrow is i−1i-1, so its anticlockwise perpendicular is i(i−1)=−1−ii(i-1)=-1-i. The two vertices are 1+i2±32(−1−i),\frac{1+i}{2}\pm\frac{\sqrt3}{2}(-1-i), or, as an unordered pair, 1+32(1+i),1−32(1+i).\boxed{\frac{1+\sqrt3}{2}(1+i),\qquad\frac{1-\sqrt3}{2}(1+i).} They lie on opposite sides of the line through 11 and ii, at equal perpendicular distances from its midpoint.

☠ Problem 3.5

Prove that a+ωb+ω2c=0a+\omega b+\omega^2c=0, for distinct a,b,ca,b,c, implies an equilateral triangle. Explain why distinctness cannot be omitted.

Show solutionHide solution

The factorisation proved in Lecture 3 gives a2+b2+c2−ab−bc−ca=(a+ωb+ω2c)(a+ω2b+ωc)=0.a^2+b^2+c^2-ab-bc-ca =(a+\omega b+\omega^2c)(a+\omega^2b+\omega c)=0. If all three coordinates equal aa, the assumed relation also holds because a(1+ω+ω2)=0a(1+\omega+\omega^2)=0, but there is only one point, not a triangle. Thus a nondegeneracy condition is necessary.

☠ Problem 3.6

The triangle with vertices 1,i,−11,i,-1 lies on the unit circle. Find its circumcentre, centroid and orthocentre, and verify the Euler-line ratio.

Show solutionHide solution

All vertices have modulus 11, so the circumcentre is o=0o=0. The centroid is g=(1+i−1)/3=i/3g=(1+i-1)/3=i/3, and the orthocentre is h=1+i−1=ih=1+i-1=i. This also matches the geometric fact that the angle at the vertex ii is right, so that vertex is the orthocentre. The distances are OG=1/3OG=1/3 and GH=2/3GH=2/3, giving OG:GH=1:2OG:GH=1:2.

☠ Problem 3.7

For a triangle with general circumcentre oo and orthocentre hh, prove that its three side midpoints lie on a circle with centre (o+h)/2(o+h)/2 and radius half the circumradius.

Show solutionHide solution

Let the circumradius be RR, and let n=(o+h)/2n=(o+h)/2. Since h=a+b+c−2oh=a+b+c-2o, n=a+b+c−o2.n=\frac{a+b+c-o}{2}. For the midpoint ma=(b+c)/2m_a=(b+c)/2 of BCBC, ma−n=o−a2,|ma−n|=|o−a|2=R2.m_a-n=\frac{o-a}{2},\qquad\left|m_a-n\right|=\frac{\left|o-a\right|}2=\frac R2. The other two side midpoints have the same distance from nn by cyclic interchange. They are therefore on the circle of centre (o+h)/2(o+h)/2 and radius R/2R/2. This circle is part of the classical nine-point-circle construction.

☠ Problem 3.8

In Napoleon's theorem, prove that the centroid of U,V,WU,V,W is the centroid of the original triangle.

Show solutionHide solution

Add the three centroid formulas: u+v+w=(a+b)+(b+c)+(c+a)2−i23((b−a)+(c−b)+(a−c))=a+b+c.\begin{aligned} u+v+w&=\frac{(a+b)+(b+c)+(c+a)}2\\ &\quad-\frac{i}{2\sqrt3}\bigl((b-a)+(c-b)+(a-c)\bigr)\\ &=a+b+c. \end{aligned} The perpendicular displacement terms cancel. Dividing by 33 proves that the centroid of U,V,WU,V,W is (a+b+c)/3(a+b+c)/3, the centroid of ABCABC.

☠ Problem 3.9

If |a|=|b|=|c|=2\left|a\right|=\left|b\right|=\left|c\right|=2 and a+b+c=0a+b+c=0, find all three side lengths. Give a proof that does not assume the triangle is already equilateral.

Show solutionHide solution

Since a+b=−ca+b=-c, we have 4=|a+b|2=8+2Re(ab‾)4=\left|a+b\right|^2=8+2\operatorname{Re}(a\bar b), so Re(ab‾)=−2\operatorname{Re}(a\bar b)=-2. Then |a−b|2=8−2(−2)=12.\left|a-b\right|^2=8-2(-2)=12. The other two squared side lengths are also 1212 by the same argument. Hence all three sides have length 232\sqrt3. The equal side lengths are a conclusion of the calculation, not an assumption.

☠ Problem 3.10

Construct regular squares externally on the sides of a nonequilateral triangle. Can the three square centres form an equilateral triangle? Explain using the polynomial QQ and the multiplier ε\varepsilon.

Show solutionHide solution

For external squares n=4n=4 and ε=cos(−90∘)+isin(−90∘)=−i\varepsilon=\cos(-90^\circ)+i\sin(-90^\circ)=-i. Then 1+ε+ε2=1−i−1=−i≠0.1+\varepsilon+\varepsilon^2=1-i-1=-i\ne0. The original nonequilateral triangle has Q(a,b,c)≠0Q(a,b,c)\ne0, so the transformation formula gives Q(u,v,w)≠0Q(u,v,w)\ne0. Thus the three square centres cannot form an equilateral triangle. The cancellation in Napoleon's theorem is specific to the cubic-root relation.

☠ Problem 3.11

The points A=−1A=-1 and B=iB=i lie on the unit circle. Reflect Z=2Z=2 in the line ABAB using z′=a+b−abz‾z'=a+b-ab\bar z. Find the perpendicular foot and verify its position on the line by ordinary coordinates.

Show solutionHide solution

Here a=−1a=-1, b=ib=i, ab=−iab=-i, and z‾=2\bar z=2. The chord reflection gives z′=a+b−abz‾=−1+i−(−i)(2)=−1+3i.z'=a+b-ab\bar z=-1+i-(-i)(2)=-1+3i. Its midpoint with z=2z=2 is f=z+z′2=1+3i2.f=\frac{z+z'}2=\frac{1+3i}{2}. The line through (−1,0)(-1,0) and (0,1)(0,1) has equation y=x+1y=x+1; the midpoint (1/2,3/2)(1/2,3/2) satisfies that equation. The joining arrow z−z′=3−3iz-z'=3-3i is perpendicular to b−a=1+ib-a=1+i, because (3−3i)/(1+i)=−3i(3-3i)/(1+i)=-3i is purely imaginary. Thus this midpoint is the perpendicular foot.

☠ Problem 3.12

Four distinct points A,B,C,DA,B,C,D lie on one circle with centre OO. Write HAH_A for the orthocentre of BCDBCD, and define HB,HC,HDH_B,H_C,H_D cyclically. Prove that the four segments AHAAH_A, BHBBH_B, CHCCH_C, DHDDH_D have the same midpoint. Explain why the four corresponding lines concur.

Show solutionHide solution

Let S=a+b+c+dS=a+b+c+d. The orthocentre formula established in this lecture applies to each triangle with the same circumcentre oo. For BCDBCD it gives hA=b+c+d−2o=S−a−2oh_A=b+c+d-2o=S-a-2o, and hence a+hA2=S−2o2.\frac{a+h_A}{2}=\frac{S-2o}{2}. If we omit BB instead, the same formula gives hB=S−b−2oh_B=S-b-2o and (b+hB)/2=(S−2o)/2(b+h_B)/2=(S-2o)/2; the calculations for C,DC,D are identical. Therefore a single point MM is the midpoint of all four segments.

We should check that these segments really determine lines. Suppose, for example, a=hAa=h_A. Move OO to the origin; then b+c=a−db+c=a-d. On the same circle the two pairs B,CB,C and A,−DA,-D would have the same midpoint (a−d)/2≠0(a-d)/2\ne0. A chord with a noncentral midpoint is uniquely determined: its supporting line is perpendicular to the radius through the midpoint, and its half-length is fixed by the radius and the midpoint's distance from OO. Thus its endpoints would have to be the same pair, contrary to AA being distinct from B,CB,C. The other cases follow cyclically. Each supporting line therefore contains its segment's common midpoint MM, so all four lines concur there.

☠ Problem 3.14

The point 11 can be described with chosen angle 00 or chosen angle 2π2\pi. Find the chosen-angle power with exponent 1/31/3 in each description. Explain why the answers may differ although both cube to 11.

Show solutionHide solution

With chosen angle 00, the selected cube root is cos0+isin0=1\cos0+i\sin0=1. With chosen angle 2π2\pi, it is cos2π3+isin2π3=−12+32i.\cos\frac{2\pi}{3}+i\sin\frac{2\pi}{3}=-\frac12+\frac{\sqrt3}{2}i. The first cubing gives a turn of 00 and the second a turn of 2π2\pi, both ending at 11. The endpoint of the original turn does not determine which third of a tracked total turn is being selected. This is why a rational power needs an angle/value convention.
