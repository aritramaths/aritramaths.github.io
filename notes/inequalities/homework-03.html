The AM–GM Inequality

12 problems with worked solutions.

💡 Study note · How to Use the Homework

First identify the structure of the expression. Do not apply a named inequality merely because it is familiar. Check the hypotheses, choose the direction of the estimate, and write the equality case.

The problems are arranged by method rather than merely by difficulty. In every solution, state the AM–GM grouping or the weights explicitly, and check that the equality conditions from different steps are compatible.

Part A: Building and Weighting the Correct Terms

☠ Problem 1

Let x1,x2,…,xn>0x_1,x_2,\ldots,x_n>0, and put G=x1x2⋯xnn.G=\sqrt[n]{x_1x_2\cdots x_n}. Prove that ∏i=1n(1+xi)≥(1+G)n.\prod_{i=1}^{n}(1+x_i)\ge(1+G)^n. Determine the equality condition.

Show solutionHide solution

Let eke_k denote the sum of all products of kk distinct members of x1,…,xnx_1,\ldots,x_n, with e0=1e_0=1. Then ∏i=1n(1+xi)=e0+e1+⋯+en.\prod_{i=1}^{n}(1+x_i)=e_0+e_1+\cdots+e_n. There are (nk)\binom nk terms in eke_k. If we multiply all those terms, each xix_i appears in exactly (n−1k−1)\binom{n-1}{k-1} of them. Therefore their geometric mean is (∏i=1nxi)(n−1k−1)/(nk)=(∏i=1nxi)k/n=Gk.\left(\prod_{i=1}^{n}x_i\right)^{ \binom{n-1}{k-1}/\binom nk} =\left(\prod_{i=1}^{n}x_i\right)^{k/n} =G^k. AM–GM applied to the (nk)\binom nk terms of eke_k gives ek≥(nk)Gk.e_k\ge\binom nk G^k. Summing this for k=0,1,…,nk=0,1,\ldots,n, ∏i=1n(1+xi)≥∑k=0n(nk)Gk=(1+G)n.\prod_{i=1}^{n}(1+x_i) \ge\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom nkG^k =(1+G)^n. Equality in the k=1k=1 estimate requires x1=x2=⋯=xnx_1=x_2=\cdots=x_n, and this condition clearly gives equality throughout.

☠ Problem 2

For positive real numbers a,b,ca,b,c, prove that a4+b4+c4≥abc(a+b+c).a^4+b^4+c^4\ge abc(a+b+c). Your proof should exhibit the AM–GM groups explicitly.

Show solutionHide solution

The useful groups are chosen so that their products become fourth powers of the three terms on the right. By AM–GM, 2a4+b4+c4≥4a8b4c44=4a2bc.2a^4+b^4+c^4\ge4\sqrt[4]{a^8b^4c^4}=4a^2bc. Cyclically, 2b4+c4+a4≥4ab2c,2c4+a4+b4≥4abc2.2b^4+c^4+a^4\ge4ab^2c, \qquad 2c^4+a^4+b^4\ge4abc^2. Adding the three inequalities gives 4(a4+b4+c4)≥4abc(a+b+c).4(a^4+b^4+c^4)\ge4abc(a+b+c). Division by 44 proves the result. Equality in all three AM–GM groups is equivalent to a=b=ca=b=c.

☠ Problem 3

For positive real numbers a,b,ca,b,c, prove that (a+b)(b+c)(c+a)≥8abc.(a+b)(b+c)(c+a)\ge8abc.

Show solutionHide solution

Apply the two-variable AM–GM inequality to the three factors: a+b≥2ab,b+c≥2bc,c+a≥2ca.a+b\ge2\sqrt{ab},\qquad b+c\ge2\sqrt{bc},\qquad c+a\ge2\sqrt{ca}. Multiplication yields (a+b)(b+c)(c+a)≥8ab⋅bc⋅ca=8abc.(a+b)(b+c)(c+a) \ge8\sqrt{ab\cdot bc\cdot ca}=8abc. All three equalities occur simultaneously exactly when a=b=ca=b=c.

☠ Problem 4

Let x,y,z>0x,y,z>0 and x+y+z=1x+y+z=1. Prove that xxyyzz≥13.x^x y^y z^z\ge\frac13.

Show solutionHide solution

The exponents themselves tell us which weights to use. Apply weighted AM–GM with weights x,y,zx,y,z to the positive numbers 1/x,1/y,1/z1/x,1/y,1/z. Since x+y+z=1x+y+z=1, x1x+y1y+z1z≥(1x)x(1y)y(1z)z.x\frac1x+y\frac1y+z\frac1z \ge \left(\frac1x\right)^x \left(\frac1y\right)^y \left(\frac1z\right)^z. Thus 3≥1xxyyzz,3\ge\frac1{x^xy^yz^z}, which is the desired inequality. Equality requires 1/x=1/y=1/z1/x=1/y=1/z, so x=y=z=1/3x=y=z=1/3.

☠ Problem 5

Let p,q>1p,q>1 satisfy 1p+1q=1.\frac1p+\frac1q=1. Prove Young's inequality xpp+yqq≥xy(x,y>0),\frac{x^p}{p}+\frac{y^q}{q}\ge xy \qquad(x,y>0), and determine the equality condition.

Show solutionHide solution

Apply weighted AM–GM to xpx^p and yqy^q with weights 1/p1/p and 1/q1/q. These weights add to 11, so xpp+yqq≥(xp)1/p(yq)1/q=xy.\frac{x^p}{p}+\frac{y^q}{q} \ge (x^p)^{1/p}(y^q)^{1/q}=xy. Equality holds if and only if xp=yqx^p=y^q.

☠ Problem 6

Find the least possible value of xy+yz+zx3,x,y,z>0,\frac{x}{y}+\sqrt{\frac{y}{z}}+\sqrt[3]{\frac{z}{x}}, \qquad x,y,z>0, and describe all equality cases.

Show solutionHide solution

Put A=xy,B=yz,C=zx3.A=\frac{x}{y},\qquad B=\sqrt{\frac{y}{z}},\qquad C=\sqrt[3]{\frac{z}{x}}. Then AB2C3=1AB^2C^3=1. We split the three summands into six terms: A+B+C=A+B2+B2+C3+C3+C3.A+B+C =A+\frac B2+\frac B2+\frac C3+\frac C3+\frac C3. By AM–GM, A+B+C≥6A(B2)2(C3)36=611086=22/33.\begin{aligned} A+B+C &\ge6\sqrt[6]{A\left(\frac B2\right)^2 \left(\frac C3\right)^3}\\ &=6\sqrt[6]{\frac1{108}} =2^{2/3}\sqrt3. \end{aligned} Equality requires A=B2=C3.A=\frac B2=\frac C3. Solving these ratio conditions gives (x,y,z)=t(1,21/33,332),t>0.(x,y,z) =t\left(1,\,2^{1/3}\sqrt3,\,\frac{3\sqrt3}{2}\right), \qquad t>0. Every such triple indeed gives equality, so the least value is 22/332^{2/3}\sqrt3.

Part B: Synthesis with Earlier Methods

☠ Problem 7

For positive real numbers a,b,ca,b,c, prove that a3bc+b3ca+c3ab≥a+b+c.\frac{a^3}{bc}+\frac{b^3}{ca}+\frac{c^3}{ab} \ge a+b+c.

Show solutionHide solution

Problem 2 already gives a4+b4+c4≥abc(a+b+c).a^4+b^4+c^4\ge abc(a+b+c). Divide by abc>0abc>0 to obtain a3bc+b3ca+c3ab≥a+b+c.\frac{a^3}{bc}+\frac{b^3}{ca}+\frac{c^3}{ab} \ge a+b+c. Equality occurs exactly when a=b=ca=b=c.

☠ Problem 8

Let a,b,c>0a,b,c>0 with a+b+c=1a+b+c=1. Prove that (1−a)(1−b)(1−c)≥8abc.(1-a)(1-b)(1-c)\ge8abc.

Show solutionHide solution

The condition a+b+c=1a+b+c=1 converts the three factors into pair sums: 1−a=b+c,1−b=c+a,1−c=a+b.1-a=b+c,\qquad 1-b=c+a,\qquad 1-c=a+b. Therefore (1−a)(1−b)(1−c)=(b+c)(c+a)(a+b)≥8abc(1-a)(1-b)(1-c)=(b+c)(c+a)(a+b)\ge8abc by Problem 3. Equality holds at a=b=c=1/3a=b=c=1/3.

☠ Problem 9

Let x1,x2,…,xn>0x_1,x_2,\ldots,x_n>0, and write S=x1+⋯+xnS=x_1+\cdots+x_n. Prove the nn-variable Nesbitt inequality ∑i=1nxiS−xi≥nn−1.\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{x_i}{S-x_i}\ge\frac{n}{n-1}.

Show solutionHide solution

The denominators suggest Engel form. Since xi/(S−xi)=xi2/[xi(S−xi)]x_i/(S-x_i)=x_i^2/[x_i(S-x_i)], ∑i=1nxiS−xi≥(x1+⋯+xn)2∑i=1nxi(S−xi)=S2S2−∑i=1nxi2.\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{x_i}{S-x_i} \ge \frac{(x_1+\cdots+x_n)^2} {\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_i(S-x_i)} =\frac{S^2}{S^2-\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_i^2}. From Cauchy–Schwarz, ∑i=1nxi2≥S2n.\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_i^2\ge\frac{S^2}{n}. Hence ∑i=1nxiS−xi≥S2S2−S2/n=nn−1.\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{x_i}{S-x_i} \ge \frac{S^2}{S^2-S^2/n}=\frac{n}{n-1}. Equality in both steps occurs exactly when x1=x2=⋯=xnx_1=x_2=\cdots=x_n.

☠ Problem 10

Let a,b,c>0a,b,c>0 with a+b+c=3a+b+c=3. Prove that 11+a2+11+b2+11+c2≥32.\frac1{1+a^2}+\frac1{1+b^2}+\frac1{1+c^2} \ge\frac32.

Show solutionHide solution

For t>0t>0, t21+t2≤t2\frac{t^2}{1+t^2}\le\frac t2 is equivalent to (t−1)2≥0(t-1)^2\ge0. Consequently, 11+t2=1−t21+t2≥1−t2.\frac1{1+t^2} =1-\frac{t^2}{1+t^2} \ge1-\frac t2. Apply this with t=a,b,ct=a,b,c and add: ∑cyc11+a2≥3−a+b+c2=32.\sum_{\mathrm{cyc}}\frac1{1+a^2} \ge3-\frac{a+b+c}{2}=\frac32. Equality holds only when a=b=c=1a=b=c=1.

Part C: Past Olympiad Problems

☠ Problem 11

IMO 1995, Problem 2. Let a,b,c>0a,b,c>0 satisfy abc=1abc=1. Prove that 1a3(b+c)+1b3(c+a)+1c3(a+b)≥32.\frac1{a^3(b+c)}+\frac1{b^3(c+a)}+\frac1{c^3(a+b)} \ge\frac32.

Show solutionHide solution

The product condition suggests taking reciprocals. Put x=1a,y=1b,z=1c.x=\frac1a,\qquad y=\frac1b,\qquad z=\frac1c. Then xyz=1xyz=1, and 1a3(b+c)=x3yzy+z=x2y+z.\frac1{a^3(b+c)} =\frac{x^3yz}{y+z} =\frac{x^2}{y+z}. Thus the required sum becomes x2y+z+y2z+x+z2x+y.\frac{x^2}{y+z}+\frac{y^2}{z+x}+\frac{z^2}{x+y}. By Engel form, x2y+z+y2z+x+z2x+y≥(x+y+z)22(x+y+z)=x+y+z2.\frac{x^2}{y+z}+\frac{y^2}{z+x}+\frac{z^2}{x+y} \ge \frac{(x+y+z)^2}{2(x+y+z)} =\frac{x+y+z}{2}. Finally, xyz=1xyz=1 and AM–GM give x+y+z≥3x+y+z\ge3. Hence the original sum is at least 3/23/2. Equality holds at x=y=z=1x=y=z=1, equivalently at a=b=c=1a=b=c=1.

☠ Problem 12

IMO 2001, Problem 2. Let a,b,c>0a,b,c>0. Prove that aa2+8bc+bb2+8ca+cc2+8ab≥1.\frac{a}{\sqrt{a^2+8bc}} +\frac{b}{\sqrt{b^2+8ca}} +\frac{c}{\sqrt{c^2+8ab}} \ge1.

Write Da=a2+8bc,Db=b2+8ca,Dc=c2+8ab,D_a=a^2+8bc,\qquad D_b=b^2+8ca,\qquad D_c=c^2+8ab, and s=a+b+cs=a+b+c. Cauchy–Schwarz first gives (∑cycaDa)(∑cycaDa)≥s2.\left(\sum_{\mathrm{cyc}}\frac{a}{\sqrt{D_a}}\right) \left(\sum_{\mathrm{cyc}}a\sqrt{D_a}\right) \ge s^2. \tag{3.2} We now estimate the second factor. Another application of Cauchy–Schwarz gives (∑cycaDa)2≤s∑cycaDa=s(a3+b3+c3+24abc).\left(\sum_{\mathrm{cyc}}a\sqrt{D_a}\right)^2 \le s\sum_{\mathrm{cyc}}aD_a =s\bigl(a^3+b^3+c^3+24abc\bigr). \tag{3.3} The six positive numbers a2b,a2c,b2a,b2c,c2a,c2ba^2b,\ a^2c,\ b^2a,\ b^2c,\ c^2a,\ c^2b have geometric mean abcabc. It follows from (3.3) that ∑cycaDa≤s2.\sum_{\mathrm{cyc}}a\sqrt{D_a}\le s^2. Substitution in (3.2) yields ∑cycaa2+8bc≥1.\sum_{\mathrm{cyc}}\frac{a}{\sqrt{a^2+8bc}}\ge1. All equality conditions agree when a=b=ca=b=c, and conversely equality in the six-term AM–GM forces a=b=ca=b=c.

Remark · About the Last Two Problems

The olympiad problems are deliberately placed after the method-building questions. In both of them AM–GM is not the first visible line: it enters after a substitution or after Cauchy–Schwarz has reduced the problem to a product comparison. This is exactly the kind of synthesis I want you to practise.

💡 Summary · What Homework 3 Was Really Testing

The recurring lesson is that AM–GM begins before the inequality sign is written. We selected subsets in Problem 1, manufactured repeated copies in Problems 2 and 6, used exponents as weights in Problems 4 and 5, and combined AM–GM with substitutions or Cauchy–Schwarz in the two olympiad problems. The equality case was the consistency check in every construction.
